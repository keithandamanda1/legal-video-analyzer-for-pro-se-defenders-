EXH-F
BHA Master Key Log
Bangor Housing Authority Master Key Log for 33 Bald Mountain Drive,
showing no key authorized to BPD on August 2, 2018
WHAT THIS PROVES: Under federal public housing law (24 C.F.R. § 966.4(d)(3)), BHA may only release a master key to law enforcement upon presentation of a valid search warrant and verification. The BHA Master Key Log shows NO key was authorized to Bangor Police Department on or around August 2, 2018.

Yet an officer used a key from his pocket to enter the apartment — ON VIDEO (EXH-E). The key was given without legal authority. Entry to the apartment was warrantless and illegal under the Fourth Amendment.

BHA's own tenant file for Petitioner was subsequently destroyed (spoliation).
Supports: Ground III (Fourth Amendment — unlawful entry) · Ground VI (IAC — failure to challenge)

EXH-I
CAD / Radio Logs — Davis Road Stop
BPD CAD and radio transmission logs showing the August 2, 2018
traffic stop on Davis Road was never dispatched through official channels
WHAT THIS PROVES: The traffic stop on Davis Road — the entire predicate for the arrest — does not appear in BPD dispatch records or radio logs. All communication between officers was conducted by personal cell phone, not police radio.

An unrecorded stop that cannot be corroborated by dispatch is a hallmark of a pretextual stop. Officers cannot later claim a specific traffic infraction when they never reported one through official channels at the time. This also confirms Officer Farrar was not present at the initial Davis Road stop — her warrant affidavit is based on events she did not witness.
Supports: Ground II (Franks Violation) · Ground III (Fourth Amendment — pretextual stop)

EXH-J
2018 Maine Drug Schedule
Maine's controlled substance schedule as it existed on August 2, 2018,
showing gabapentin was NOT a scheduled substance
WHAT THIS PROVES: Petitioner was charged with trafficking in gabapentin for conduct occurring on August 2, 2018. Gabapentin was not added to Maine's controlled substance schedule (Schedule Z) until October 15, 2019, by Me. PL 2019, ch. 487.

A person cannot be convicted for conduct that was not criminal at the time it occurred. The gabapentin charge was void ab initio — a pure ex post facto violation under U.S. Const. art. I, § 10 and Me. Const. art. I, § 11.

This count must be dismissed regardless of the outcome of all other grounds. No hearing is required — it is a pure legal defect on the face of the record.
Supports: Ground VII (Gabapentin — Ex Post Facto / Legally Void Charge) — AUTOMATIC DISMISSAL
✔ IN HAND
Source: Maine Legislature — official drug schedule, pre-October 2019 version
