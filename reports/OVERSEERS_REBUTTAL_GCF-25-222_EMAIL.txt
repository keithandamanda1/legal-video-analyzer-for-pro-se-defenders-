-----------------------------------------------------------------------
I. BOARD'S FACTUAL ERROR ON GABAPENTIN — REQUIRES CORRECTION AS A
   THRESHOLD MATTER
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The dismissal (Page 3) states:

  "Contrary to your suggestion, Gabapentin is a Schedule Z drug in
   Maine and was so at the time of your arrest."

This statement is factually incorrect as a matter of law.

Gabapentin was NOT a scheduled controlled substance in Maine on
August 2, 2018 — the date of the arrest. Gabapentin was added to
Maine's Schedule Z by:

  Maine Public Law 2019, Chapter 487
  "An Act To Schedule Gabapentin as a Schedule Z Controlled Substance"
  Effective date: October 15, 2019

The legislative history is publicly available from the Maine
Legislature's online database. The effective date is unambiguous:
more than fourteen months AFTER the conduct charged.

This is not a close legal question. A conviction for conduct that was
not criminal at the time of occurrence violates the Ex Post Facto
Clause, U.S. Const. art. I, § 10; Me. Const. art. I, § 11.
See Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37 (1990); Rogers v. Tennessee,
532 U.S. 451 (2001).

Charging a defendant with gabapentin trafficking for conduct in August
2018 was legally impossible — and defense counsel's failure to raise
this meritorious, dispositive issue constitutes ineffective assistance
of counsel on its face. Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668 (1984).

A charging decision based on a legally impossible charge is not a
reasonable exercise of prosecutorial discretion under MRPC 3.8(a); it
is a failure of basic legal competence.

The Board is requested to correct this error of law in any
reconsideration.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
II. THE BOARD IGNORED THE STATE'S OWN WRITTEN ADMISSION OF FABRICATED
    AGGRAVATION — THE CORE EVIDENCE IN THE FILE
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The dismissal's analysis of Count 2 makes no mention whatsoever of
the November 6, 2025 letter from current AAG Jason D. Anton, Esq. —
the same attorney copied on the dismissal letter itself.

That letter is in this Board's file. It constitutes the State of
Maine's own written admission that the Aggravated Trafficking charges
were based on an "inadvertent error" regarding the location of
evidence — the claim that drugs and firearms were found together in
the vehicle.

  STUVER'S CLAIM (July 15, 2025 Response):
  "Three counts of Aggravated Trafficking...were based on the weight
   of the heroin and two firearms found in Mr. King's vehicle."

  STATE'S OWN INDICTMENT (Ex. C, Counts 9-10, Place of Seizure):
  "33 Bald Mountain Drive, Bangor, Maine" — the RESIDENCE, not
  the vehicle.

  STATE'S ADMISSION (Anton letter, November 6, 2025):
  The claim that drugs were found with firearms in the vehicle was an
  "inadvertent error."

Maine's Aggravated Trafficking statute, 17-A M.R.S.A. § 1105-A(1)
(C-1), requires the firearm be possessed "in furtherance of" the
trafficking offense — a nexus requiring proximity. Drugs in an
apartment and firearms in a vehicle on Davis Road satisfy no such
nexus. The aggravation factor was legally unsupportable from the
start.

The Board's dismissal states "there was no evidence presented to
support the allegation that the indictment was drafted maliciously."
This finding cannot stand when the Board's own file contains a letter
from the State's current prosecutor admitting the factual predicate
for the most serious charges was erroneous. That is not "no evidence"
— that is a smoking gun.

MRPC 3.8(a) prohibits a prosecutor from "institut[ing] criminal
charges that the prosecutor knows or reasonably should know are not
supported by probable cause." The Board failed to analyze this.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
III. STUVER MADE A FALSE STATEMENT TO THIS BOARD — DIRECTLY WITHIN
     SCOPE
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The dismissal (Page 5) states:

  "Attorney Stuver states that she is not aware of any search of
   Ms. Ross's cellphone that occurred."

The Search Warrant issued August 7, 2018 (Docket No. SW-18-180,
Maine District Court, Southern Penobscot Division) — cited by
Stuver's own counsel — explicitly lists as a target:

  "Black LG Touch screen smartphone MEID (D) #A100003E98DD95. This
   phone is owned by Amanda Ross and currently held at Bangor Police
   Department."

Stuver's counsel cannot simultaneously cite this warrant as evidence
of lawful procedure and allow her to claim she is "not aware of any
search of Ms. Ross's cellphone."

One of two things is true:
  (a) Attorney Stuver is not aware of the warrant she oversaw —
      raising serious competence concerns under MRPC 1.1; or
  (b) Attorney Stuver made a false statement to Bar Counsel — a
      direct violation of MRPC 8.4(c).

Either way, this statement warranted scrutiny. Bar Counsel accepted
it without examination.

Furthermore, the Ross phone was seized August 2, 2018 — before ANY
warrant authorized it. The August 7 warrant does not retroactively
legalize that initial warrantless seizure. Wong Sun v. United States,
371 U.S. 471 (1963). Stuver, as supervising prosecutor, was
responsible for that chain of custody.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
IV. THE VIDEO TIMELINE IS IRRECONCILABLE WITH THE DISMISSAL'S FINDING
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The dismissal (Page 2) states Stuver made video available "within two
weeks" of the arrest.

The full dashcam video was not produced until February 21, 2024 —
five and a half years after the arrest — confirmed via FOAA request.
The footage shows:

  — No traffic infraction articulated at the stop
  — No warrant shown at the apartment door despite two direct requests
  — Officers producing a key from their own pocket to enter
  — Three-hour detention in 90-degree heat, injury without medical care
  — Dashcam metadata (timestamp, GPS, officer ID) completely stripped

Selective disclosure of partial video while withholding complete
footage for 5.5 years is itself a Brady violation. Brady v. Maryland,
373 U.S. 83 (1963). This is not addressed in the dismissal.

Stripping metadata from a dashcam file is an affirmative act — not an
absence of proof — and it is documented. Where a prosecutor controls
production of evidence, the burden to demonstrate completeness rests
with the State.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
V. THE BOARD FAILED ENTIRELY TO ADDRESS THE CONFLICT-OF-INTEREST
   ALLEGATION
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The complaint raised, with supporting documentation (October 2025),
that Attorney Stuver had previously represented DHHS/CPS in a related
family matter involving Keith King while the criminal case was
simultaneously pending.

This implicates MRPC 1.7 (Conflict of Interest: Current Clients)
and/or MRPC 1.9 (Duties to Former Clients), as well as the
heightened duty of a prosecutor under MRPC 3.8.

The dismissal does not address this allegation anywhere in its
twelve-point analysis. This is a material omission that warrants
review.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
VI. THE BOARD'S PERSONAL ATTACK ON COMPLAINANT'S SUBMISSIONS IS
    IMPROPER AND DOES NOT SUBSTITUTE FOR LEGAL ANALYSIS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The dismissal (Page 2, footnote 2) twice references the Complainant's
possible use of an AI content generator, implying the submissions are
less credible.

Bar Counsel states she "reviewed your allegations under all applicable
rules of professional conduct, regardless of whether they were
accurately cited." If that is true, the source of the citations is
irrelevant to the outcome.

Pro se complainants may use any lawful tool to prepare submissions.
Penalizing a disabled, unrepresented complainant for using assistive
technology while allowing Respondent to be represented by a licensed
attorney (Jason Anton, Esq.) is a structural imbalance — not a
neutral observation.

The core factual contradictions — drug location, firearms location,
gabapentin scheduling date, phone warrant timeline — are established
by the face of the indictment, the police reports, and the State's
own subsequent admissions. No AI is required to identify them.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
VII. THE SCOPE LIMITATIONS WERE APPLIED OVERBROAD AND SHIELDED THE
     MOST SERIOUS CONDUCT FROM REVIEW
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Bar Counsel's scope limitation to "allegations against Attorney
Stuver" was then used to exclude Stuver's own direct conduct:

  — Stuver's false statement to this Board (Sec. III) is Stuver's
    own conduct before this very tribunal — directly within scope.
  — Stuver's indictment based on a nexus she later admitted was
    erroneous — directly within scope under MRPC 3.8(a).
  — Stuver's gabapentin charge under a law not yet in effect —
    directly within scope under MRPC 3.1 and MRPC 3.8(a).
  — Stuver's undisclosed DHHS/CPS conflict — directly within scope
    under MRPC 1.7 and 3.8.

None of these require analysis of third-party conduct. The Board's
scope limitations should not have prevented their review.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
VIII. THE BAIL ANALYSIS IGNORES ITS OWN LOGICAL IMPLICATION
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The dismissal states the 93% bail reduction ($75,000 to $5,000) does
not establish anything inappropriate with the initial amount.

This is circular reasoning. A judicial reduction of 93% on a motion
challenging excessiveness is judicial validation of the excessiveness
claim. The original bail was premised on the inflated Class A
Aggravated Trafficking charges that the State later admitted were
based on an "inadvertent error." When the factual predicate for the
most serious charges was erroneous, the bail premised on those charges
was correspondingly unsupported.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
IX. STUVER'S DEPARTURE IN DECEMBER 2019 DOES NOT INSULATE HER FROM
    RESPONSIBILITY FOR CONDUCT THAT OCCURRED WHILE SHE WAS THE
    PROSECUTOR
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The complaint does not allege Stuver was present at the 2021 plea.
It alleges that Stuver's filing of false Aggravated Class A charges —
based on a nonexistent firearm-drug nexus — created the coercive
pressure her successor exploited to threaten a four-year mandatory
minimum and force the plea. Stuver built the weapon; Horn fired it.

An attorney who files charges they know or should know are unsupported
by probable cause cannot avoid professional responsibility for the
downstream consequences simply by leaving the office before the
resulting coercion takes effect.
