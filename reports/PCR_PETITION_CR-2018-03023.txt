                        INTRODUCTION

Petitioner Keith A. King respectfully petitions this Court for post-conviction

                        STATEMENT OF FACTS

1. ARREST AND STOP (August 2, 2018)

On August 2, 2018, at approximately 12:56 PM, Officer Evan Haskell and Officer
Perry stopped Petitioner on Davis Road, Glenburn, Maine. The dashcam video
(released February 21, 2024 — 5.5 years after the arrest) shows:

    a. No traffic infraction was articulated.
    b. No warrant was in hand at the time of the stop.
    c. Petitioner was handcuffed and transported from Davis Road to his
       residence — an "off-site transport" of a "recent occupant" not authorized
       by the residence warrant. See Bailey v. United States, 568 U.S. 186 (2013).
    d. Petitioner asked for a warrant TWICE at the apartment door; both times
       officers refused to show one. Officers produced a key from their pocket
       and entered.
    e. The search warrant application was signed approximately 2 hours AFTER
       the traffic stop — meaning the entire initial detention was warrantless.
    f. The traffic stop was never logged in dispatch or radio — an off-book stop.
    g. Dashcam metadata (date, time, GPS coordinates, officer ID) was stripped
       from the produced footage — evidence of tampering.

2. CHAIN OF CUSTODY VIOLATION

    a. Evidence was held by Officer Gastia in an unrecorded location for 18
       hours overnight before being logged.
    b. The TruNarc field test for the alleged heroin returned "INCONCLUSIVE."
       No confirmatory laboratory test was completed. Charges of "in fact heroin"
       were filed without scientific basis.
    c. Gabapentin was charged as a controlled substance for August 2, 2018 conduct.
       Gabapentin was not scheduled in Maine until October 15, 2019.
       See Me. PL 2019, ch. 487 (eff. Oct. 15, 2019). The charge was legally
       impossible.

3. PHONE SEIZURES (August 2–September 2018)

    a. The search warrant contained an explicit exclusionary clause prohibiting
       seizure of cellular phones. Despite this, officers seized Petitioner's phone.
    b. The phone was held in an evidence locker August 2–7 without a warrant.
    c. A second warrant (August 7) was obtained to search the already-illegally-
       seized phone. This warrant is tainted fruit. Wong Sun v. United States,
       371 U.S. 471 (1963).
    d. Amanda Ross's phone was seized for approximately 45 days without a warrant.
       The DA stated it was never taken; forensic records prove it was extracted.
    e. 1,000+ calls and 350+ private photos were extracted from multiple devices.
       Private materials have since appeared on the internet.

4. COERCED PLEA (September 21, 2021)

    a. A prior court order required medical stabilization before any plea.
    b. The September 21, 2021 hearing was calendared for a mental-health/
       medication check-in, NOT a plea.
    c. AAG Jason Horn arrived at the hearing and presented a surprise plea offer
       with a 30-minute ultimatum. Petitioner was unmedicated and in mental
       health crisis.
    d. Horn threatened a 4-year mandatory minimum if the offer was refused.
    e. Court-appointed counsel did not seek a continuance or disability
       accommodations despite Petitioner's documented mental health conditions
       (Bipolar Disorder, Panic Attack Disorder).
    f. Counsel filed no suppression motions, no Franks challenge, no drug-testing
       challenge, and did not enforce Brady/Rule 16 obligations.
    g. The factual basis for "in fact heroin" was never established; the science
       was inconclusive.

5. NEWLY DISCOVERED EVIDENCE (Post-Conviction)

    a. December 17, 2025: Former AAG Janice Stuver formally admitted the
       Aggravated Class A charges were based on an "inadvertent error" regarding
       the location of evidence.
    b. February 21, 2024: Dashcam video released — 5.5 years after arrest.
       The footage confirms: no warrant shown, officers had keys, three-hour
       detention in 90-degree heat while Petitioner complained of injured wrist
       and was denied medical attention.
    c. October 2025: BHA Master Key Log (Exhibit B, MHRC complaint) produced.
       The log contains ZERO entry for August 2, 2018. No key was officially
       checked out. Officers obtained and used Petitioner's master key entirely
       outside the official checkout system — a covert, unauthorized handoff with
       no paperwork. Petitioner's December 2025 PCR affidavit confirms this explicitly.
       The absence of any entry is itself the evidence of the off-book arrangement.
    d. October 5, 2025: Confirmation that AAG Stuver had previously represented
       DHHS/CPS in a related family matter while this criminal case was pending —
       a conflict of interest never disclosed.
    e. HUD FOIA (Exhibit L, MHRC complaint): Internal emails reveal that HUD
       officials Kara E. Norman and Richa Karki (Boston Regional Office) coordinated
       with BHA counsel Bethany and BrHA Director Perkins to ignore Petitioner's
       records requests — an inter-agency cover-up.

                        GROUNDS FOR RELIEF

GROUND 1: INVOLUNTARY PLEA — BOYKIN v. ALABAMA / M.R.U. CRIM. P. 11(b)(3)

The September 21, 2021 plea was not knowing, intelligent, or voluntary:
    • Surprise plea at a hearing scheduled for mental-health review
    • Petitioner unmedicated and in documented mental health crisis
    • 30-minute ultimatum with 4-year threat
    • No adequate explanation of rights being waived
    • Factual basis for "in fact heroin" was unsupported (TruNarc inconclusive)
    • Firearm aggravator lacked required "in furtherance" nexus

Authorities: Boykin v. Alabama, 395 U.S. 238 (1969); Brady v. United States,
397 U.S. 742 (1970); M.R.U. Crim. P. 11(b)(3).

GROUND 2: INEFFECTIVE ASSISTANCE OF COUNSEL — STRICKLAND / HILL / LAFLER

Counsel's performance was deficient and prejudicial. Specifically, counsel:
    (1) Filed no motion to suppress the initial pretextual traffic stop
        (no articulable basis, unrecorded stop — Rodriguez v. United States,
        575 U.S. 348 (2015))
    (2) Filed no motion challenging off-site transport/detention
        (Bailey v. United States, 568 U.S. 186 (2013))
    (3) Filed no Franks challenge despite nonexistent/contradictory "witness
        statements" cited in the affidavit (Franks v. Delaware, 438 U.S. 154)
    (4) Failed to challenge phone seizure that violated warrant's explicit
        exclusionary clause
    (5) Failed to move for independent drug testing or challenge inconclusive
        TruNarc results
    (6) Failed to move to dismiss the gabapentin charge (not scheduled in Maine
        until October 2019)
    (7) Failed to challenge the multiplicitous OAS counts
    (8) Failed to challenge the firearm aggravator's lack of nexus
    (9) Did not enforce Brady/Rule 16 — withheld dashcam produced 5.5 yrs late
    (10) Did not seek continuance or disability accommodations at surprise plea

But for these failures, there is a reasonable probability Petitioner would
have rejected the plea and prevailed at suppression. Hill v. Lockhart, 474 U.S.
52 (1985); Lafler v. Cooper, 566 U.S. 156 (2012); Missouri v. Frye, 566 U.S.
134 (2012).

GROUND 3: BRADY / GIGLIO / NAPUE VIOLATIONS

The prosecution withheld or misrepresented:
    • Inconclusive TruNarc heroin test results
    • Full dashcam footage (withheld 5.5 years)
    • Dashcam metadata/audit logs (still missing)
    • Amanda Ross phone seizure and forensic extraction (DA falsely denied)
    • Nonexistent or contradictory "witness statements" in warrant affidavit
    • AAG Stuver's conflict of interest (CPS overlap)

Authorities: Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83 (1963); Giglio v. United States,
405 U.S. 150 (1972); Napue v. Illinois, 360 U.S. 264 (1959); Kyles v. Whitley,
514 U.S. 419 (1995); Ferrara v. United States, 456 F.3d 278, 290-95
(1st Cir. 2006) (Brady violations that materially influence a plea warrant vacatur).

GROUND 4: FOURTH AMENDMENT — UNLAWFUL STOP, SEARCH, AND SEIZURE

    a. Stop without articulated basis, unrecorded, warrant signed 2 hours post-stop
    b. Off-site transport/detention without warrant authority (Bailey)
    c. Residential entry with keys provided by BHA without warrant or consent
       (4th Amendment; 24 C.F.R. § 966.4(d)(3); 5 M.R.S. § 4582)
    d. Phone seized despite explicit warrant exclusion
    e. Amanda Ross phone seized without any warrant for 45 days
    f. All downstream evidence is tainted fruit (Wong Sun)

GROUND 5: LEGALLY IMPOSSIBLE GABAPENTIN CHARGE

Gabapentin was not a scheduled controlled substance in Maine on August 2, 2018.
It was added to Schedule W by Me. PL 2019, ch. 487, effective October 15, 2019.
A conviction cannot rest on conduct that was not criminal at the time.
This ground requires dismissal of the gabapentin count regardless of other
relief. See U.S. Const. art. I, § 10 (Ex Post Facto Clause).

GROUND 6: COERCIVE PRETRIAL CONDITIONS — PLEA INVOLUNTARINESS CONTEXT

    • $100,000 cash-only bail for a first-time, non-violent, disabled defendant
      with $370, no passport, lifelong Bangor ties; parents' $450K home + 45 acres
      + ankle monitoring denied
    • Judicial comment at bail: "selling drugs that are killing people" — pre-verdict
    • No-contact with spouse (80% of mental health support) despite observed
      panic attacks
    • Medication denied for 42 days of detention
    • Transfer to Two Bridges Correctional (100 miles) — punitive, no reason given
    • These conditions collectively coerced the plea

GROUND 7: EQUITABLE TOLLING

    • Petitioner was actively stonewalled by BHA, BPD, and state agencies for
      over seven years
    • Key evidence (dashcam, BHA key log, AAG admissions, HUD FOIA records)
      was not available until 2024–2025
    • All claims are timely under § 2128-B (newly discovered evidence pathway)
    • Fraudulent concealment tolls limitations under 14 M.R.S. § 859
