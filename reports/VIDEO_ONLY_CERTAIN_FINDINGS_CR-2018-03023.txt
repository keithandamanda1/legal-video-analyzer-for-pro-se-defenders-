────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FINDING 1 — HANDS-ON PAT-DOWN / PHYSICAL CONTROL OF SUBJECT AT REAR OF
VEHICLE (CERTAIN — confirmed across 4 consecutive sampled frames)
Timestamps: 00:03:20, 00:03:40, 00:03:50, 00:04:00 (continuous sequence)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Across four sampled frames spanning 40 seconds, the officer is in continuous physical
contact with the subject at the rear of the stopped vehicle: hands on the subject's
body in pat-down position (00:03:20, 00:03:40), subject's arm positioning consistent
with a control hold (00:03:50), continuing at 00:04:00. One frame could be ambiguous;
a 40-second continuous sequence is not.

What this proves with certainty:
  • A hands-on physical search/frisk of the subject's body occurred.
  • The subject was physically detained (seized) at the rear of the vehicle.

What it does NOT prove by itself: whether the frisk was legally justified. A Terry
frisk requires reasonable articulable suspicion the person is ARMED AND DANGEROUS —
that justification must appear in the officer's report, or the frisk was unlawful.

Law: Terry v. Ohio, 392 U.S. 1 (1968); 4th Amendment; Maine Const. Art. I §5.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FINDING 2 — SUBJECT REMOVED FROM / OUTSIDE VEHICLE, DETAINED ON ROADWAY
(CERTAIN — confirmed across multiple frames)
Timestamps: 00:03:30, 00:04:10, 00:04:30, 00:04:40
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The subject is outside the vehicle and held at the rear of the truck on the open
roadway across multiple consecutive sampled frames. At 00:03:30 the subject is
holding/displaying items outside the vehicle. This is a sustained roadside detention,
not a brief license-and-registration exchange.

What this proves with certainty:
  • This was a full detention/seizure of the person, not a routine document check.
  • The encounter escalated to subject-out-of-vehicle, hands-on control.

Law: 4th Amendment (seizure); Pennsylvania v. Mimms, 434 U.S. 106 (1977) (exit
order allowed, but escalation beyond it requires justification); Rodriguez v.
United States, 575 U.S. 348 (2015) (stop cannot be prolonged beyond its mission).

FINDING 3 — VEHICLE'S REAR/TRUNK AREA AND BOTH SIDES ACCESSED BY OFFICERS
(CERTAIN AS EVENT — confirmed across 5 consecutive sampled frames)
Timestamps: 00:00:10, 00:00:20, 00:00:30, 00:00:40, 00:00:50
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Five consecutive sampled frames (50 seconds of continuous footage) show the officers
positioned at, leaning into, and examining the rear/trunk area and both sides of the
stopped vehicle. One ambiguous frame is a guess; fifty continuous seconds of officers
physically engaging the vehicle's compartments is a vehicle examination/search.

What this proves with certainty:
  • Officers physically engaged and examined the vehicle, including the rear/trunk
    area, in the opening minute of this segment.

What it does NOT prove by itself: whether consent, probable cause, or a warrant
existed at that moment. NOTE: If the warrant was signed ~2 hours after the stop,
no warrant could have authorized this examination at the time shown.

Law: 4th Amendment; Arizona v. Gant, 556 U.S. 332 (2009); Carroll v. United
States, 267 U.S. 132 (1925); Maine Const. Art. I §5.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The footage also contains a residential scene: an officer positioned at or just

FINDING 8 — METADATA MISSING FROM THE ORIGINAL FILE AS RECEIVED FROM POLICE
(CONFIRMED BY KEITH — based on the original February 2024 production, NOT the
trimmed segments)
====================================================================================================
Keith confirms the metadata (date, time, GPS, officer/unit identifiers) was ALREADY
MISSING from the original video file as received from the police/DA in February
2024 — before he made any trimmed segments. His trimming did not cause this.

What this supports:
  • Standard Maine cruiser video carries date/time/GPS/unit data by default. Its
    absence from the State's own production means the file the State produced
    cannot establish WHEN the stop occurred — the exact fact in dispute (stop at
    ~10:30 AM vs. warrant signed ~12:30 PM).
  • The stripping/withholding of that data is a Brady and evidence-integrity issue
    chargeable to the State, which controls the original recording system.

How to make this bulletproof: preserve the original medium (disc/USB/download)
exactly as received; state in an affidavit that the metadata was absent AS RECEIVED
and that any segments were trimmed by Keith afterward only for analysis.

Law: Brady v. Maryland (1963); M.R.E. 901(a); 18 U.S.C. §1519; 17-A M.R.S.A. §453.
