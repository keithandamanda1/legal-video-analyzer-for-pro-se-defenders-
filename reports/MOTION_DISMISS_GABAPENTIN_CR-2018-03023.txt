                        INTRODUCTION

Defendant Keith A. King respectfully moves this Court to dismiss the count
charging Defendant with unlawful trafficking in gabapentin arising from
conduct on August 2, 2018.

The reason is simple and dispositive: gabapentin was NOT a scheduled
controlled substance in Maine on August 2, 2018. It was not added to
Maine's Schedule W until October 15, 2019 — more than fourteen months
AFTER the alleged conduct. Charging Defendant for conduct that was not
criminal at the time it occurred violates the Ex Post Facto Clause of the
United States Constitution and the Maine Constitution.

                        LEGAL STANDARD

The Ex Post Facto Clause, U.S. Const. art. I, § 10, prohibits laws that
"retroactively alter the definition of crimes or increase the punishment
for criminal acts." Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37, 42 (1990).
The Maine Constitution provides identical protection. Me. Const. art. I, § 11.

A conviction for an act that was not criminal at the time of the conduct
is void ab initio. See Rogers v. Tennessee, 532 U.S. 451, 456 (2001).

                        FACTS

1. The charged conduct occurred on August 2, 2018.

2. Gabapentin (pregabalin/gabapentin) was NOT scheduled as a controlled
   substance in Maine on August 2, 2018.

3. The Maine Legislature added gabapentin to Schedule W by:
   Maine Public Law 2019, Chapter 487
   An Act to Schedule Gabapentin as a Schedule W Controlled Substance
   Effective date: October 15, 2019

4. The charge of trafficking in gabapentin for August 2, 2018 conduct
   therefore rests on a law that did not exist at the time of the act.

5. Charging Defendant retroactively under a law enacted 14+ months after
   the conduct constitutes an ex post facto application of law.

                        ARGUMENT

I. THE GABAPENTIN CHARGE IS LEGALLY VOID UNDER THE EX POST FACTO CLAUSE.

Under Maine law, a person may only be criminally prosecuted for conduct that
was prohibited at the time it was performed. Charging Defendant with trafficking
gabapentin for conduct in August 2018 — when gabapentin was not a controlled
substance in Maine until October 2019 — is a textbook ex post facto violation.

This is not a close case. There is no ambiguity about:
    (a) The date of the alleged conduct: August 2, 2018
    (b) The date gabapentin was scheduled in Maine: October 15, 2019
    (c) The legal consequence: the charge was legally impossible

II. DEFENSE COUNSEL'S FAILURE TO RAISE THIS ISSUE CONSTITUTES IAC.

This defect was facially apparent from the face of the charging document.
Defense counsel's failure to move for dismissal of this count constitutes
ineffective assistance of counsel under Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S.
668 (1984). Counsel was deficient in failing to identify and raise a
meritorious, dispositive legal argument.

The prejudice is clear: Petitioner was pressured to plead guilty in part
because of the multiplied charges and associated mandatory minimums, at least
one of which was legally impossible from the outset.

III. DISMISSAL IS REQUIRED — NO CURE IS POSSIBLE.

Unlike many defects, an ex post facto violation cannot be cured or excused.
The charge must be dismissed with prejudice. No amendment or re-indictment
can create a valid charge for conduct that occurred 14 months before the
substance was scheduled.

                        RELIEF REQUESTED

Defendant respectfully requests that this Court:

    1. DISMISS WITH PREJUDICE the count charging Defendant with trafficking
       in gabapentin arising from conduct on August 2, 2018;

    2. FIND that defense counsel's failure to raise this dispositive issue
       constitutes ineffective assistance of counsel;

    3. Consider this dismissal as an independent basis, or contributing basis,
       for vacatur of the plea and full post-conviction review.
