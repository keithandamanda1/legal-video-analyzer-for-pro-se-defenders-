Petition for Post-Conviction Review. In support, Petitioner states:

1. I am the Petitioner in the above-captioned matter.

2. I have nine (9) documented mental health disabilities. I am permanently disabled
   and unable to maintain full-time employment.

3. My sole income is Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) in the amount of
   approximately $996.00 per month.

4. I have no significant assets, savings, stocks, bonds, or property that could be
   liquidated to pay filing fees.

5. After paying for basic necessities (housing, food, utilities, medications), I have
   no disposable income.

6. Requiring payment of filing fees would constitute an undue financial hardship and
   would effectively deny me access to the courts to address serious constitutional
   violations established by newly discovered evidence obtained in 2024–2025.

   access to its courts solely because of inability to pay fees when fundamental
   rights are at stake. Boddie v. Connecticut, 401 U.S. 371 (1971).

8. Additionally, my nine documented mental health disabilities entitle me to
   reasonable accommodations in these proceedings under the Americans with
   Disabilities Act, 42 U.S.C. § 12132, and the Maine Human Rights Act,
   5 M.R.S. § 4592. I respectfully request that this Court note my disability
   status and provide appropriate accommodations including, but not limited to,
   additional time to respond to any court orders.

    1. GRANT this Motion to Proceed In Forma Pauperis and waive all filing fees;

If anyone tells you this is "too late" — your response is:

1. The newly discovered evidence (dashcam Feb 2024, BHA key log Oct 2025,
   HUD FOIA records 2025, AAG admission Dec 2025) was not available until
   2024-2025. The clock starts when evidence becomes available, not from
   the date of conviction. 15 M.R.S. § 2128-B.

2. Your nine mental health disabilities prevented timely filing. This is
   equitable tolling. Maine courts recognize disability as grounds to excuse
   late filing. 14 M.R.S. § 859.

   lied about Amanda Ross phone). Fraudulent concealment also tolls the

================================================================================
                         AFTER YOU FILE — WHAT HAPPENS
================================================================================

1. The court will rule on your IFP motion (usually within a few weeks).
2. If granted, your PCR petition is accepted for filing at no cost.
3. The court will set a hearing date.
4. At the hearing, you can request appointed counsel (15 M.R.S. § 2129(1)(B)).
5. The State (AG's office) will respond to your petition.

You do NOT need an attorney to file. You have the right to file pro se.
