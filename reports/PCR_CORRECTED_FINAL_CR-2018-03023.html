Part IV: Grounds for Relief

Ground I: Brady/Napue Violation—State's Admitted False Charge Basis

Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83 (1963); Napue v. Illinois, 360 U.S. 264 (1959); Ferrara v. United States, 456 F.3d 278 (1st Cir. 2006)

The State charged Petitioner with three Class A counts of Aggravated Trafficking by falsely merging two legally distinct locations:

THE TRUTH:

  • Firearms: Davis Road, vehicle, locked cases, plain sight, bill of sale dated morning of arrest
  • Drugs: 33 Bald Mountain Drive, apartment
  • THESE ARE LEGALLY DISTINCT LOCATIONS, LEGALLY DISTINCT SEIZURES, LEGALLY DISTINCT OFFENSES. The 17-A M.R.S. § 1105-A "in furtherance of" nexus requires contemporaneous co-location of drugs and firearm. That nexus never existed. Officer Haskell's report confirms no drugs in vehicle. Officer Perry found only a holster in apartment.

STATE'S ADMISSION: Current AAG Jason D. Anton, in response to Bar Counsel (Board of Overseers Case GCF #25-222, April 2026), acknowledged the merged gun-drug narrative was an "inadvertent error." The State of Maine, through its current prosecutor, has admitted in writing that the most serious charges—used to drive $100,000 bail and coerce the plea—were factually impossible.

ADDITIONAL BRADY VIOLATION: The TruNarc field test for heroin returned an INCONCLUSIVE RESULT. No confirmatory laboratory analysis was produced. The factual basis for the "in fact heroin" plea was never scientifically established, yet Petitioner pled to a Class A heroin possession charge.

Under Ferrara, a plea obtained through false charges must be vacated where the misconduct materially influenced the decision to plead. The merged narrative drove the bail, the pretrial conditions, and the plea ultimatum.

Ground II: Franks Violation—False and Impossible Warrant Affidavit

Franks v. Delaware, 438 U.S. 154 (1978)

A. Non-Existent Witness Statements — Primary Franks Basis

The affidavit cited "witness statements" as a basis for probable cause. The State's own discovery checklist explicitly marks these statements as "DOES NOT EXIST." Officer Farrar swore to witness statements she had not reviewed and which the State now admits do not exist. Under Franks, the deliberate or reckless inclusion of non-existent evidence in a probable cause affidavit is a material misrepresentation entitling Petitioner to suppression of all evidence obtained under the warrant.

B. Impossible Timeline—Corroborating the Falsity

The warrant affidavit was signed at 12:00 PM on August 2, 2018. The CAD log establishes Officer Farrar did not arrive at the residence until 12:56 PM—56 minutes after filing the affidavit. She could not have personally reviewed or verified any information about conditions at the residence when she signed. This timeline impossibility corroborates that the affidavit was submitted before any investigation of the residence occurred.

C. Relief

If the State cannot establish that the affidavit was accurate or that any misrepresentations were neither deliberate nor reckless, all evidence obtained under the warrant—including the drugs seized at 33 Bald Mountain Drive—must be suppressed. Suppression of the only evidence supporting the conviction warrants vacatur of the plea.

The traffic stop on Davis Road does not appear in Bangor Police Department dispatch records. No articulable traffic infraction or reasonable suspicion is documented. FRUIT: Any seizure of firearms during this undocumented stop is fruit of the poisonous tree and must be suppressed.

Petitioner was handcuffed at the Davis Road location and transported to his residence at 33 Bald Mountain Drive to execute a residential search warrant. Bailey v. United States, 568 U.S. 186, 201–03 (2013) expressly prohibits this practice. FRUIT: All evidence discovered at the residence is suppressible fruit of this unlawful transport under Wong Sun v. Officer Perry used a master key to enter 33 Bald Mountain Drive. The Bangor Housing Authority's master key log shows that NO MASTER KEY WAS ISSUED TO BANGOR POLICE DEPARTMENT ON AUGUST 2, 2018. Entry was unannounced. Petitioner asked to see the warrant twice; both times officers said "We will get to that" and used the key to enter. THIS IS ON VIDEO.

D. Warrantless Public Strip Search

Amanda Ross was subjected to a warrantless, public, male-officer-conducted strip search on Davis Road without probable cause and without a female officer present. This is an unreasonable Fourth Amendment search under any standard.

RELIEF: All evidence obtained through this chain of Fourth Amendment violations must be suppressed under the fruit-of-the-poisonous-tree doctrine. Suppression of the only evidence supporting the conviction warrants vacatur of the plea.

Ground V: Involuntary Plea—Violation of Due Process and Prior Court Order

Boykin v. Alabama, 395 U.S. 238 (1969); M.R. Crim. P. 11(b)(3); M.R.U. Crim. P. 32(d); Ferrara v. United States, 456 F.3d 278 (1st Cir. 2006)

A guilty plea is constitutionally valid only if knowing, voluntary, and intelligent. Post-sentence plea withdrawal requires a showing of manifest injustice under M.R.U. Crim. P. 32(d). The September 20, 2021 plea satisfies neither standard:

A. Violation of Prior Court Order

On July 29, 2021, this Court ordered that Petitioner receive mental health treatment and achieve stable medication dosage BEFORE ENTERING ANY PLEA. Petitioner attended the September 20, 2021 hearing unmedicated, in direct violation of this order. Counsel did not invoke the prior order or seek continuance. A plea taken in violation of a court's own protective order is manifest injustice.

B. Ambush Plea—No Notice

The hearing was scheduled for mental health/medication compliance review, not a plea hearing. Petitioner had no notice a plea offer would be presented. No continuance was provided.

C. Coercive Ultimatum

AAG Jason Horn presented a take-it-or-leave-it plea offer with a 30-minute deadline, threatening a mandatory 4-year sentence if rejected. This constitutes duress. The 4-year threat was based on the Aggravated Class A charges—now admitted by the State to have been based on an "inadvertent error." The coercive lever was factually impossible.

D. Failure to Seek ADA Accommodation

All counsel possessed actual knowledge of Petitioner's nine documented mental health disabilities. None sought continuance or accommodation for an unmedicated client presented with a coercive ultimatum. This violates the Americans with Disabilities Act, 42 U.S.C. § 12132, and the Maine Human Rights Act, 5 M.R.S. § 4592.

E. Defective Factual Basis

The factual basis for the plea was legally defective:

  • The Aggravated charges providing coercive leverage were based on a false merged narrative, now admitted by the State
  • The heroin charge was based only on an inconclusive TruNarc result with no confirmatory laboratory analysis
  • The Gabapentin charge was legally impossible—the substance was not scheduled in Maine until October 15, 2019, fourteen months after the conduct charged

Under Ferrara, a plea induced by withheld exculpatory evidence or false charges must be vacated where the misconduct materially influenced the decision to plead. The cumulative effect of all five involuntariness factors constitutes manifest injustice under Rule 32(d).

Ground VI: Ex Post Facto Violation—Gabapentin Charge Must Be Dismissed

U.S. Const. art. I, § 10; Me. Const. art. I, § 11; Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37 (1990)

Gabapentin was not a scheduled controlled substance in Maine on August 2, 2018—the date of the charged conduct. It was added to Schedule Z by Me. PL 2019, ch. 487, effective October 15, 2019—more than fourteen months after the arrest. A conviction cannot rest on conduct that was not criminal at the time it occurred. This violates the Ex Post Facto Clause of both the United States and Maine Constitutions. EXH-J (2018 Maine Drug Schedule) confirms gabapentin's absence from the controlled substances list on the date of arrest. This count must be dismissed regardless of all other relief requested.
