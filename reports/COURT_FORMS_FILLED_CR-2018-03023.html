C. Grounds for Relief (15 M.R.S. § 2125)
CAUTION: If you fail to set forth any grounds for relief in your Petition, you may be prevented from presenting them at a later date. Petitioner sets forth four primary grounds below. Additional grounds (Ex Post Facto — Gabapentin; Fourth Amendment Bailey Violation; ADA Accommodation Denial) are stated in full in the verified Petition and Memorandum attached hereto and incorporated by reference.
1. Ground One:
Brady v. Maryland / Napue v. Illinois — State's Written Admission of Fabricated Charges (Involuntary Plea — False Factual Basis)
Supporting FACTS:
On November 6, 2025, current AAG Jason D. Anton admitted in writing to the Maine Board of Bar Overseers (GCF #25-222) that the factual basis for all three Class A Aggravated Trafficking charges — asserting that firearms from the Davis Road vehicle stop were co-located with drugs from the Bald Mountain apartment — constituted an "inadvertent error." Those false Class A charges, threatening a mandatory 4-year minimum, were the coercive lever for Petitioner's plea. The State's own discovery checklist marks cited "witness statements" as "DOES NOT EXIST." Petitioner's plea was induced by charges the State now admits had no factual basis — a Brady violation and a Napue false-evidence violation. See Exhibit C (Anton letter, Nov. 6, 2025); Exhibit B (2018 initial discovery); full argument in attached Petition.
2. Ground Two:
Fourth Amendment / Franks v. Delaware — Materially False Warrant Affidavit; Unauthorized Master Key Entry
Supporting FACTS:
The residential search warrant affidavit was signed by Officer Shannon Ferrah at 12:00 PM on August 2, 2018. BPD CAD/Radio logs (obtained January 2026) confirm Ferrah did not arrive at 33 Bald Mountain Drive until 12:56:10 PM — 56 minutes after swearing to personal observations impossible at that time. Additionally, entry was made using a Bangor Housing Authority master key: the BHA Master Key Log contains no authorized entry for August 2, 2018 (Exhibit F). No warrant was shown despite two demands on video (Exhibit E). The warrant was facially invalid; all evidence seized under it is suppressible fruit. See Exhibits A, B, E, F, I, and attached Petition.

3. Ground Three:
Ineffective Assistance of Counsel (Strickland v. Washington) — All Four Attorneys Concealed Exculpatory Video; Billed State for Review; Filed No Motions
Supporting FACTS:
Four successive court-appointed attorneys — Martha Harris (Bar #156), William Johnson (Bar #6302), Ezra A. R. Willey, and William G. Bart — each reviewed police cruiser video for CR-2018-03023 and billed the Maine Commission on Indigent Legal Services for that review (Exhibit G: billing vouchers, obtained August 2024 from Audit Director Stephen Brochu). Each attorney affirmatively represented to Petitioner that the video did not exist. Petitioner obtained the video February 21, 2024 — 5.5 years post-arrest. Within the first 30 minutes, the video documents: an undisputed illegal traffic stop (no articulable infraction); handcuffing and off-site transport in violation of Bailey v. United States, 568 U.S. 186 (2013); master-key entry without knock-and-announce; officer's refusal to produce warrant on two demands; and an unconsented public strip search of Petitioner's wife. Zero suppression motions were ever filed. Under Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668 (1984), both prongs are satisfied. See Exhibits E and G; full argument in attached Petition.
4. Ground Four:
Involuntary Plea (Boykin v. Alabama / M.R.U. Crim. P. 11) — Plea Obtained While Unmedicated, in Violation of Court Order, Under 30-Minute Ultimatum, Without Factual Basis
Supporting FACTS:
The September 20/21, 2021 hearing was calendared as a mental health and medication compliance status conference — not a plea hearing. A prior court order (July 29, 2021) required medical stabilization before any plea could be accepted. Petitioner was unmedicated for 42+ days in direct violation of that order, suffering from nine documented mental health disabilities (Bipolar Type 2, ADHD, Panic Attack Disorder, Anxiety Disorder, Major Depression, Narcolepsy, Epilepsy, and others; Exhibit K). AAG Jason Horn presented a surprise plea offer with a 30-minute ultimatum and threatened a mandatory 4-year sentence if refused. Counsel (Bart) did not seek a continuance, did not invoke the court's prior order, did not request an ADA accommodation. The TruNarc field test for the charged substance returned "INCONCLUSIVE" (Exhibit H) — the plea to "in fact heroin" (Class C) lacked the factual basis required by M.R.U. Crim. P. 11(b)(2). The plea was constitutionally void ab initio. See Exhibit K; full argument in attached Petition.
